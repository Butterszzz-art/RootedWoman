Resources · Nutrition Guide

Nutrition Basics

Simple habits that make a real difference. No calorie counting. No complicated plans. No perfection required.

You do not need a perfect diet. You need a sustainable one. Read through the whole guide first. Then pick two or three habits to start with. Add more as those become natural.

Section 1 — Your Quick Wins

Start here. These habits alone will change how you feel. Highest impact, lowest effort — no special food required.

💧
Quick Win 1 — Drink a minimum of 2 litres of water every day

Most people are dehydrated and don't know it. Fatigue, headaches, hunger, poor focus, and cravings are often simply your body asking for water. Start your morning with a large glass before coffee or food. Keep a bottle visible at your desk. Finish 1.5 litres before 12pm — the rest comes easily. Want a personal target? Multiply your body weight in kg by 40ml.

🥤
Quick Win 2 — Eliminate sugary drinks and sodas

Sodas, fruit juices, energy drinks, sweetened coffees, and sports drinks are some of the biggest sources of hidden sugar. They add calories without making you feel full, and they spike your blood sugar, which leads to energy crashes and cravings. Replace with water, sparkling water, unsweetened herbal tea, or black coffee. If this feels hard, start by eliminating one per day and build from there.

🍔
Quick Win 3 — Reduce junk food and fast food

Fast food is engineered to make you want more — this is not a willpower problem. You don't need to eliminate it forever. If you currently eat fast food 5 times per week, reduce to 3. Then 2. Then 1. Small reductions done consistently beat dramatic cuts that don't last.

🍳
Quick Win 4 — Reduce fried food

Frying dramatically increases calorie content and introduces fats that contribute to inflammation and low energy. When you cook at home, choose grilling or baking. When eating out, choose grilled where possible. Make a better choice more often than not.

🛒
Quick Win 5 — Reduce processed and packaged food

A simple rule: if a product has more than 5 ingredients, or ingredients you can't pronounce, eat it less often. Replace packaged snacks with whole foods: fruit, nuts, boiled eggs, vegetables with hummus.

🥣
Quick Win 6 — Eat a real breakfast

Skipping breakfast sets your blood sugar on a rollercoaster for the rest of the day. A breakfast with protein keeps you fuller longer and gives you stable energy. Easy options: 2 eggs with vegetables, Greek yoghurt with fruit, oats with nuts, or a protein smoothie.

🥩
Quick Win 7 — Include protein in every meal

Protein is the building block of muscle. If you're training, your body needs it to recover and grow stronger — and it keeps you fuller for longer, reducing cravings. Good sources: chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yoghurt, legumes, cottage cheese, tofu. You don't need to count grams — just make sure it's on the plate.

🥦
Quick Win 8 — Add vegetables to at least two meals per day

Vegetables keep your digestive system healthy, help you feel full, and stabilise blood sugar. You don't need to eat salad every day. Add spinach to eggs, put cucumber and tomato on the side of your lunch, roast a tray of vegetables for dinner. Small additions add up.

Section 3 — Reduce or Avoid

Not forever. Just less often than before. Progress is the goal, not perfection.

x Sugary drinks and sodas — Fanta, Pepsi, Coca-Cola, fruit juice, energy drinks, sweetened iced tea
x Fried food — anything deep-fried or pan-fried in large amounts of oil
x Junk food and fast food — burgers, hot dogs, chips, fried chicken, pizza
x Packaged and processed snacks — crisps, cookies, flavoured crackers, instant noodles
x Excess sugar — sweets, desserts, sugary cereals, flavoured yoghurts with added sugar
x Alcohol — it disrupts sleep, recovery, and hormonal balance. If you drink, drink less.

A useful question before eating: Does this food give me energy, or take it from me? Answer honestly and choose accordingly.

Section 4 — Practical Tips

Knowing what to eat is the easy part. These tips bridge the gap between knowing and doing.

🥡 Meal preparation

You don't need to meal prep for hours on Sunday. Even 30 minutes makes a difference. Boil eggs, wash and cut vegetables, cook a batch of rice. Having ready food removes the decision and the temptation.

🛒 Never shop hungry

Grocery shopping on an empty stomach leads to buying food you didn't plan to buy. Eat first, then shop. And write a list before you go.

⚖️ The 80/20 approach

Eat well 80% of the time. The other 20% is life — a birthday, a dinner out, a craving. This is not failure. This is balance. Make a good choice more often than a poor one.

🔍 Read labels

Start noticing the sugar content on labels. If sugar is listed in the first three ingredients, or if the product has more than 10g of sugar per 100g, choose a different option or eat it less often.

🍽️ Eat slowly

Your brain takes about 20 minutes to register that your stomach is full. Put down your fork between bites. Eat without your phone. Chew properly. These small things make a significant difference over time.

🕐 Don't skip meals

Skipping meals to compensate slows your metabolism, increases cravings, and almost always leads to overeating later. Eat regular meals. Keep portions sensible. Stay consistent.

Section 5 — Training & Food

What you eat before and after training changes everything. Food is not separate from your training — it is part of it. Eating enough of the right food is not a reward for training. It is a requirement for training to work.

The Pre-Workout Meal — eat 1 to 1.5 hours before training
🔥
Training on an empty stomach burns muscle, not fat

Without fuel, your body turns to muscle tissue for energy — a process called catabolism. The result: lower strength, faster-dropping endurance, earlier breakdown in form, slower recovery, and the muscle you're working to build gets used as fuel instead.

🍜
Carbohydrates are your fuel

Carbs are stored in muscles and liver as glycogen and released as glucose during training. Without enough beforehand, you'll feel heavy, flat, and weak — not because you're unfit, but because you're unfuelled. Good sources: oats, rice, sweet potato, whole grain bread, banana.

🥩
Protein protects your muscle

Pre-workout protein doesn't fuel the session — carbs do that — but it protects muscle from being broken down and starts recovery before the session is even finished. Good sources: chicken breast, fish, eggs, Greek yoghurt, whey protein.

⏰
Timing

Eat a solid meal with protein and carbs 1–1.5 hours before training. No time for a full meal? A light snack 30–60 minutes before — a banana with a little protein, or a protein shake — is better than nothing. Avoid high-fat, high-fibre food right before training; it slows digestion and can cause discomfort mid-session.

✓Chicken breast + brown rice + green salad (preferred)
✓Eggs on sourdough toast + a piece of fruit
✓Oats with protein powder stirred in + a banana
✓Greek yoghurt + oats + berries
✓Tuna + rice cakes + a small handful of nuts
The Post-Workout Meal — eat within 60 to 90 minutes after training
🔬
The most important nutritional moment of your day

In the 60–90 minutes after training, muscle cells are highly sensitive to insulin and the demand for amino acids to begin repair is at its highest. Eat well in this window and you accelerate recovery, reduce soreness, and build stronger muscle. Skip it or eat poorly, and you leave the training process incomplete.

💪
Protein is non-negotiable

Protein provides the amino acids your body uses to repair and rebuild muscle fibres broken down during training. Aim for a post-workout meal with 25–40g of protein. Good sources: salmon, chicken breast, white fish, tuna, or a quality whey protein shake.

⚡
Carbohydrates restock what you used

Glycogen used during training gets replenished by post-workout carbs — directed straight to the muscle, not stored as fat. Restricting carbs after training out of fear of weight gain is counterproductive: you're not eating extra, you're replacing what the session used. Good sources: brown or white rice, sweet potato, regular potato, oats, beans.

✓Salmon + brown rice + zucchini
✓Chicken breast + sweet potato + a large green salad
✓Tuna + white rice + steamed broccoli
✓Lean ground beef + brown rice + green beans
✓White fish + potatoes + spinach
Micronutrients — the details that support everything
🥦
Magnesium

Supports muscle contraction and relaxation, reduces cramps, improves sleep. Leafy greens, nuts, seeds, dark chocolate, legumes.

🩸
Iron

Carries oxygen to your muscles. Low iron is a common cause of fatigue during training. Red meat, spinach, lentils, fortified cereals — pair with vitamin C to absorb better.

☀️
Vitamin D

Supports muscle function, bone strength, and immunity. Many women are deficient. Fatty fish, eggs, fortified dairy, and sunlight.

🦴
Calcium

Essential for muscle contractions and bone density — increasingly important as you train and age. Dairy, fortified plant milks, almonds, leafy greens.

🍌
Potassium

Regulates fluid balance and prevents cramps during and after training. Bananas, sweet potato, spinach, avocado, coconut water.

Many women have been told that eating less is always better — that food is something to be earned, controlled, or feared. Eating the right food in the right quantities at the right time is one of the most powerful tools you have for changing your body, your energy, and your health. It is not indulgence. It is strategy. The Rooted Woman does not fear food. She uses it.

"You do not need to eat perfectly.
You need to eat better than you did yesterday."

My First Day — A note from your coach

I remember walking through those doors like it was yesterday. I had talked myself into it for weeks. Put on the most presentable workout clothes I owned — nothing matched, nothing felt right — and walked in. And then I stopped.

In front of me were women who looked like they had been sculpted. Toned arms, visible abs, coordinated sets. They moved through the gym like they owned it. I felt myself shrinking. I looked down at what I was wearing. I looked at my body. I thought — I do not belong here. These women are already everything I want to be. And I am starting from zero.

Then I looked around at the equipment. Machines I had never seen before, cables going in directions I could not figure out. And then I saw the men — big, muscular, lifting weights I could not even imagine picking up. I wanted to leave.

But I stayed. And what I know now — what I wish someone had told me that day — is this: every single person in that gym was a beginner once. The difference between her and me in that moment was not talent, or genetics, or money. It was time. She had simply been doing this longer than I had.

The men who made me feel like I did not belong? They were not thinking about me at all. People in the gym are almost always too focused on their own training to pay attention to anyone else. And the women I wanted to be? I became one. Not overnight. But over time, with consistency, with the courage to keep showing up even on the days I felt out of place — I became her. You will too.

That is why I created this guide. Not to give you a perfect plan, but to give you what I wish someone had given me on that first day — the truth about what it feels like to begin, and the knowledge that beginning is the bravest thing you can do. — Shadey

Section 1 — You Belong Here

What to do when the gym feels terrifying. This is one of the most common — and least talked about — experiences a beginner has. Naming it is the first step to moving past it.

👯
The women you are comparing yourself to

Every woman whose body looks the way you want yours to look started exactly where you're standing right now. Their body is the result of time and consistency — not a standard you need to meet before you're allowed to be there. Stop comparing your beginning to someone else's middle.

🏋️
The big muscular men

They are almost certainly not paying attention to you — most serious gym-goers are absorbed in their own training. You have every right to be there. The free weights, the squat rack, the cable machines — all of it is for you too. Take your space.

🎛️
Not knowing how to use the machines

Every machine has a label or diagram on the side — start there, then try it with light weight to get the feel. If you still can't figure it out, ask a trainer or staff member. It takes about thirty seconds, and even gym-goers with ten years' experience have to work out unfamiliar equipment.

👀
When you feel people are watching

They are probably not — and even if someone glances over, they're seeing a woman working on herself. That feeling fades with time. After a few weeks, the gym starts to feel familiar. Put your headphones in, follow your programme, and let the rest fade into the background.

The matching workout set, the perfect hair, the confidence — none of that makes her workout better than yours. You showing up in whatever you have is already everything. And it is always OK to ask a trainer how a machine works — that is smart, not embarrassing. You will get a better result and avoid injury.

Section 2 — Before You Train

What to do before every session.

🏃
Always warm up — no exceptions

Begin every session with 10 minutes of light cardio — a brisk walk, a gentle cycle, or a slow jog. This raises your heart rate and prepares your joints for movement. Training a cold body is how injuries happen.

💧
Drink water before you start

Come to the gym already hydrated. Drink at least one large glass of water before you leave home, and keep a bottle with you throughout your session.

🍜
Don't train on an empty stomach

You don't need a big meal, but you need something — a banana, a small bowl of oats, or a yoghurt 30–60 minutes before you train. Training fasted often leads to dizziness, fatigue, and early burnout in the session.

📄
Know your session before you walk in

Read through your training schedule the night before or on the way there. Walking in without a plan leads to wandering, wasted time, and the temptation to give up when you can't figure out what to do next.

Section 3 — Posture

How to stand, move, and hold yourself correctly. Poor posture doesn't just produce worse results — it causes injury.

Neutral spine is the most important concept in training. It means your back is in its natural gentle S-curve — not arched excessively, not rounded or hunched. To find it: stand tall, pull your shoulders back and down, keep your chest open, and imagine a straight line from the crown of your head to your tailbone.

✅ Correct posture

Chest open and lifted · Shoulders back and down · Gentle natural curve in the lower back · Core lightly engaged · Head in line with spine

❌ Common mistakes

Shoulders rolled forward · Lower back excessively arched · Upper back rounded or hunched · Head jutting forward · Chest collapsed inward

Posture during exercises — the most common beginner errors
Squats and lunges

The back rounds forward as you lower. Keep your chest up and spine neutral throughout. If your back rounds, the weight is too heavy or your mobility is limited — reduce the weight and work on depth gradually.

Pressing movements (shoulder press, chest press)

The lower back excessively arches to compensate for tight shoulders. Keep the core engaged and the ribs down.

Pulling movements (rows, lat pulldowns)

The shoulders roll forward at the end of the movement. Focus on pulling the shoulder blades together and keeping the chest open throughout.

If something feels wrong in your back during an exercise, stop. Reset your posture. Start lighter. Your spine is not worth the extra weight on the bar.

Section 4 — Core Engagement

The core is the entire system of muscles that stabilises your trunk — deep abdominals, the muscles around your spine, your pelvic floor, your diaphragm, and your glutes. It is the foundation of every movement your body makes.

1Take a breath in and let your belly expand naturally.
2As you exhale, imagine you're bracing for a light punch to the stomach — not sucking in, but stiffening from the inside out.
3Hold that gentle stiffness without holding your breath.
4That is an engaged core. Practice this standing still before you apply it to exercises.

Keep your core engaged throughout every exercise — not just the hard part. Before you lift, engage. Before you squat, engage. Before you press, engage. Make it a habit before it becomes automatic.

Section 5 — Breathing

The most overlooked technique in training. Holding your breath increases blood pressure, reduces performance, causes dizziness, and can lead to injury.

The basic rule: exhale on the effort, inhale on the release. The effort is the hardest part of the movement — the push, the pull, the drive upward. That is when you exhale.

During a squat

Inhale as you lower down. Exhale as you push back up to standing.

During a row

Inhale as you extend the arms forward. Exhale as you pull the weight toward you.

During a hip thrust

Inhale at the bottom. Exhale as you drive the hips up.

During a shoulder press

Inhale as you lower the weight. Exhale as you press it overhead.

Never hold your breath during an exercise. If you find yourself holding your breath, the weight is likely too heavy or the movement is too unfamiliar. Reduce the weight and reset.

Section 6 — Form and Technique Over Weight

The most important rule in the gym: perfect form with light weight will always produce better results — and fewer injuries — than poor form with heavy weight.

🎯
Why form matters more than weight

When form breaks down — too heavy, too fatigued, not yet learned — other muscles compensate and the joints absorb the stress instead. A perfectly executed hip thrust with 20kg does more for your glutes than a sloppy one with 60kg, every time.

📈
When you're ready to increase weight

When you can complete every rep of every set with full control and correct form, without straining. If you're grinding out the last reps with compromised posture or momentum, you're not ready — stay at the current weight until it feels solid.

⚠️
The ego trap

Lifting a weight that's too heavy because you want to look like you can handle it — or because the person next to you is lifting heavier. Nobody is impressed by heavy weight lifted with poor form, and the injury it causes can keep you out of the gym for months.

The small plate rule: when you increase weight, increase by the smallest amount available. Small, consistent increases over time produce far greater results than ego-driven jumps that compromise your form.

Section 7 — Mind-Muscle Connection

The ability to consciously focus on and feel the muscle you're supposed to be working. Intentional focus on the target muscle during training increases its activation and produces better results.

Before you start a set, place your hand lightly on the muscle you're about to work — your glutes for a hip thrust, your lats for a lat pulldown. Feel where it is, then consciously squeeze it during the movement. Slow the rep down to give yourself more time to feel it. Quality over quantity — ten reps where you genuinely feel the muscle working beats twenty reps where you're just moving the weight from A to B.

Section 8 — Muscle Burn vs Pain

Know the difference. Respect both. This is one of the most important sections in this guide — please read it carefully.

🔥 Muscle burn — continue

Heat inside the muscle · Fatigue and discomfort · Fades quickly after the set · In the belly of the muscle · Normal and expected — this is the discomfort that produces results

⚠️ Pain — stop immediately

Sharp or shooting sensation · Joint discomfort · Persists after you stop · Feels wrong · Never push through this

If you feel pain — stop immediately. Do not push through it. Do not finish the set. Rack the weight, rest, and assess. If it persists, do not continue training that day and consult a professional. There is no rep, no set, and no weight that is worth an injury that takes you out of the gym for months. Your body is communicating with you. Listen to it.

Section 9 — Rest, Hydration & After Training

The work does not stop when you leave the gym.

⏱️
Rest between sets

Rest is part of the training, not laziness. As a beginner, rest 60–90 seconds between sets for most exercises, and 90–120 seconds for heavier compound movements like squats, hip thrusts, and deadlifts. Use the time to breathe, check your form, and prepare for the next set.

💧
Hydration during training

Drink consistently throughout your session, not just when you feel thirsty — by the time thirst hits, you're already mildly dehydrated. Aim for small sips every 10–15 minutes. Dizziness, headache, cramps, or sudden fatigue mean stop, sit down, and drink water.

🧘
After your session

Cool down with 5–10 minutes of light movement and stretch the major muscle groups you worked. Eat within 60–90 minutes — a meal with protein and carbohydrates supports recovery and muscle development.

🤝
Gym etiquette — the basics

Wipe down every machine or bench after use. Re-rack your weights exactly where you found them. Don't sit on a machine between sets if others are waiting. Ask before working in on a machine someone else is using.
